    UNDER THE HOOD

    How a photo and a situation
    become a meme.

    Nothing starts from a blank page. We look for real captions and meme pictures that already worked, find the closest ones in five different ways, and only then ask Gemini to adjust them to you.

    The data

    1,500 meme templates (picture, name, number of text boxes) and 18,360 real captions that people wrote on them, each with its author and upvotes. Every caption and every picture was converted once into a list of 768 numbers, an embedding, by Gemini Embedding 2. Text and pictures live in the same space, so a sentence can be compared directly with a picture. Things with similar meaning land close together, and closeness is measured by cosine similarity (1 means pointing the same way, 0 means unrelated).

    What is matched to what

    SearchYour inputCompared withHowGood at finding
    1Your situation’s wordsThe text of every real caption (and template names)BM25 word overlap, ignoring openers like “WHEN YOU…”Exact wording: “light”, “roommate”
    2Your situationAll 18,360 real captionsCosine similarity of embeddings: meaning, not wordingCaptions about the same kind of moment
    3Your situationThe 1,500 meme picturesCosine similarity between a sentence and a pictureReaction faces that express the feeling
    4Your photoThe meme picturesCosine similarity between two picturesSimilar scenes and poses
    5Your photoThe real captionsCosine similarity between a picture and a sentenceCaptions that describe a similar scene

    Each search ranks the templates. A template’s score in a search is its best picture or caption in that search.

    From five rankings to eight candidates

    The rankings are merged by rank (reciprocal rank fusion) with hand-set weights: words 1.0, situation↔captions 1.0, photo↔pictures 0.75, situation↔pictures 0.5, photo↔captions 0.4. The pool of eight is filled round-robin, one slot at a time from each search, so no search can crowd the others out. Inside a template, captions are ordered by closeness to your situation, with a small bonus for upvotes as a tie-breaker.

    Gemini adapts, it does not invent

    Gemini sees your photo (if you gave one), your situation, and the eight candidates with three real captions each. For every option it must pick one candidate and one real caption, then adapt it: keep the structure and wording where they still fit, change only what is needed, use details from your photo, and treat the situation as the reason behind the photo. The server checks every option. Each card credits the real caption and its author and shows which searches found it.

    Worked example: working in the dark when the light goes on

    One real run: the photo shows a person hunched over a laptop with a jacket over their head, next to a whiteboard; the situation is “When you are working in the dark for hours and your roommate turns on the bright light in the room”. Top three templates per search, with scores:

    1. 1 · words ↔ caption words: disappearing (22.6) · WHICH SIDE ARE YOU ON? (14.6) · What if you-But god said (13.6)
    2. 2 · situation ↔ captions: Limmy waking up (0.88) · disappearing (0.83) · Black guy disappearing (0.82)
    3. 3 · situation ↔ pictures: blinking guy vertical blank (0.40) · I’ll Just Wait Here (0.39) · screaming seagull (0.39)
    4. 4 · photo ↔ pictures: Busy (0.651) · Monkey Business (0.651) · Computer Guy Facepalm (0.650)
    5. 5 · photo ↔ captions: neo dodging a bullet (0.408) · Busy (0.406) · tired cat (0.405)

    Merged pool, best first: disappearing (searches 1, 2) · Limmy waking up (2) · Black guy disappearing (2) · Busy (4, 5) · WHICH SIDE ARE YOU ON? (1) · blinking guy (3) · neo dodging a bullet (5) · Monkey Business (4).

    Gemini then adapted the real caption “Me: Turns on light / Monsters in the room:” (141 upvotes) into “Roommate: *turns on the bright overhead light* / Me under my jacket at 2 AM:”, using the jacket from the photo and the light from the situation.

    What this can and cannot do

    • Measured: on 2,500 captions held out of the data, the right template appears in the top 20 of 1,500 about 41% of the time (random would be about 1%).
    • Not yet measured: whether people find these funnier than a plain AI caption (the 1 to 5 buttons are there to find out). The two photo searches have not been tested separately, and as the example shows their scores are nearly tied, so they mostly add variety. The weights are intuition, not tuned.
    • Upvotes are only a rough stand-in for “went viral”, and the collection leans toward older, popular templates.
    • Privacy: your photo goes to Gemini only. The matcher receives your words and numbers, never the image, and nothing is saved.
